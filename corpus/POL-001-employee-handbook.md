# Employee Handbook Overview and Employment Classifications

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

## 1. Purpose of the Handbook

This handbook is the starting point for every Fernhill Technologies policy. It explains how our policies fit together, defines the employment classifications that other policies rely on, and tells you where to go when you have a question.

The handbook does not replace the detailed policies. Each topic, such as paid time off, expenses or data security, has its own policy document with its own identifier (for example POL-002 for the Paid Time Off Policy). When you need a precise rule, read the detailed policy.

## 2. How Policies Fit Together

### 2.1 Policy identifiers

Every policy has an identifier in the form POL-NNN, a version number, an owner and an effective date. Cite the identifier and section number when you ask a question or raise a ticket. This makes it much faster for People Operations to help you.

### 2.2 Order of precedence

Policies are written to agree with each other. If two documents appear to conflict, apply these rules in order:

1. Local law always takes priority over any Fernhill policy.
2. A detailed topic policy takes priority over this handbook. For example, the Expense Reimbursement and Travel Policy (POL-007) takes priority over any summary of expense rules in this handbook.
3. If two detailed policies conflict, the one with the later effective date takes priority.
4. If you are still unsure, raise a ticket with People Operations under the category "Policy Question". Do not guess.

Any apparent conflict should be reported to People Operations so the documents can be corrected.

### 2.3 Policy facts and recommendations

Policies state rules. They do not tell you what decision is best for your personal situation. When People Operations staff, managers or the HR assistant give you guidance, they should make clear which parts are policy facts (what the document says) and which parts are recommendations (a suggested course of action). Recommendations are never binding.

## 3. Employment Classifications

Other policies refer to the classifications below. Your classification is recorded in your employee profile.

### 3.1 Full-time employee

A full-time employee is hired on an ongoing basis and is scheduled to work 40 hours per week. Full-time employees are eligible for all benefits and programs described in Fernhill policies, subject to the waiting periods in each policy.

### 3.2 Part-time employee

A part-time employee is hired on an ongoing basis and is scheduled to work fewer than 40 hours per week. Part-time employees scheduled for 20 or more hours per week are eligible for prorated paid time off and for certain benefits, as described in POL-002 and POL-009. Part-time employees scheduled for fewer than 20 hours per week accrue prorated paid time off but are not eligible for company health plans.

### 3.3 Intern

An intern is engaged for a fixed term of up to 6 months as part of a learning program. Interns are paid, receive company holidays, and accrue sick leave under POL-010. Interns do not accrue paid time off and are not eligible for company health plans, retirement matching or stipends.

### 3.4 Independent contractor

An independent contractor provides services under a contract for services and is not an employee. Contractors are paid according to their contract. Contractors do not accrue paid time off, do not receive paid company holidays, and are not eligible for company benefits, stipends or the Work From Anywhere program. Contractors must still follow the Information and Data Security Policy (POL-006) and the Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy (POL-012). Questions about a contractor's terms are answered by the contract and the contractor's Fernhill sponsor, not by employee policies.

### 3.5 Classification changes

A change in classification, such as an intern converting to a full-time employee, takes effect on the date recorded by People Operations. Waiting periods in other policies are measured from the employee's original start date unless the policy says otherwise.

## 4. Probationary Period

### 4.1 Length

Every new full-time and part-time employee has a probationary period of 90 calendar days, starting on the first day of employment. Interns and contractors do not have a probationary period.

### 4.2 What changes during probation

During the probationary period:

- Paid time off accrues but generally cannot be taken in the first 60 calendar days. See POL-002 section 5.
- The employee is not eligible for the Work From Anywhere program. See POL-005 section 3.
- The home office stipend is not yet available. See POL-007 section 6.
- The retirement plan employer match has not yet started. See POL-009 section 5.

Health coverage is not tied to probation. It starts on the first day of the month following the start date, as described in POL-009.

### 4.3 Extension

A manager may extend probation once, by up to 30 calendar days, with written approval from People Operations. The employee must be told in writing before the original 90 days end.

## 5. Working Hours and Core Hours

### 5.1 Standard hours

The standard working week is 40 hours, Monday to Friday. Each team agrees its own schedule with its manager.

### 5.2 Core hours

To make collaboration across time zones possible, every employee is expected to be available during core hours of 10:00 to 15:00 in the time zone of their assigned office, unless their manager has agreed a different arrangement in writing.

### 5.3 Overtime

Employees who are eligible for overtime under local law must have overtime approved by their manager in advance. Overtime rules differ by country and by state or province. People Operations answers overtime questions individually. The HR assistant does not calculate overtime pay.

## 6. Offices and Countries of Employment

Fernhill Technologies employs people in three countries and has three offices:

| Office | Country | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Denver | United States | Headquarters |
| Toronto | Canada | Engineering and customer success hub |
| Lisbon | Portugal | European hub |

Your country of employment is the country where Fernhill employs you and runs your payroll. It is recorded in your employee profile. Many rules, including holidays, leave and tax, depend on your country of employment and not on where you happen to be working on a given day.

## 8. Where to Get Help

### 8.1 The HR assistant

The HR assistant is an AI tool that answers policy questions and helps with routine requests. It answers only from published Fernhill policies and from your own employee record. It always cites the policy and section it relied on. It cannot approve requests, change records or make decisions on behalf of a manager or People Operations. Any ticket or message it prepares is a draft until you confirm it.

### 8.2 Your manager

Your manager is the first point of contact for scheduling, time off approvals, workload and day-to-day questions.

### 8.3 People Operations

People Operations owns every policy in this handbook. Contact them through the HR portal by raising a ticket, or by email at people@fernhill.example. Response times are set out in POL-013.

### 8.4 Sensitive matters

For harassment, discrimination, safety concerns, medical matters or anything you do not want to discuss with your manager, go directly to People Operations or use the anonymous Ethics Line described in POL-012. You never need your manager's permission to do so.

## 9. Changes to Policies

People Operations reviews every policy at least once a year. Changes are announced by email and in the HR portal at least 14 calendar days before they take effect, unless a change is required sooner by law. The current version of each policy is the one published in the HR portal.
