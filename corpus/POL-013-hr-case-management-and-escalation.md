# HR Case Management and Escalation Procedure

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure explains how questions and requests to People Operations are handled: the ticket categories, the response times, which matters must be handled by a person and never by the HR assistant, and how a case is escalated. It applies to all tickets raised in the HR portal by employees and interns.

## 2. How to Raise a Ticket

Raise a ticket in the HR portal. Choose the category that best fits your question. You may also ask the HR assistant, which can answer many policy questions immediately and can prepare a draft ticket for you. A draft ticket is not submitted until you confirm it.

Each ticket receives a ticket number and is assigned a priority (section 4). You can see the status of your own tickets in the HR portal.

## 3. Ticket Categories

| Category | Used for | Handled by |
|---|---|---|
| Policy Question | General questions about what a policy means | People Operations generalist |
| PTO Request | Requests for paid time off and balance questions | Manager, then People Operations if needed |
| Leave Request | Sick, medical, parental, bereavement and unpaid leave | People Operations case manager |
| Benefits Question | Eligibility, elections and qualifying life events | Benefits specialist |
| Work Arrangement Change | Moving between hybrid and fully remote, or moving home | Manager, director and People Operations |
| Work From Anywhere | Working temporarily from another state, province or country | Manager and People Operations |
| Expense Question | Reimbursement rules and claim problems | Finance |
| Payroll Question | Pay, deductions, tax forms and bank details | Payroll specialist |
| Onboarding | Problems in a new employee's first weeks | People Operations generalist |
| Accommodation Request | Adjustments for a disability, medical condition or religious practice | People Operations case manager |
| Workplace Concern | Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation and safety | People Operations case team only |

## 4. Priorities and Response Times

### 4.1 Priorities

| Priority | Meaning | First response | Target resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Urgent | Safety risk, harassment or discrimination concern, or a pay error affecting the current pay period | 4 business hours | Depends on the case |
| P2 High | A decision is needed within 5 business days, such as imminent leave or travel | 1 business day | 3 business days |
| P3 Standard | All other requests | 2 business days | 5 business days |

### 4.2 Categories with fixed priority

Workplace Concern tickets are always P1. Accommodation Request and Leave Request tickets are at least P2. Onboarding tickets are P2 and People Operations aims to resolve them within 1 business day. All other categories default to P3 unless the dates in the ticket make them P2.

### 4.3 Business hours

Business hours are 09:00 to 17:00, Monday to Friday, in the time zone of the employee's assigned office, excluding the company holidays for the employee's country of employment (POL-003).

## 5. Matters That Must Be Handled by a Person

### 5.1 The rule

Some matters are too sensitive, too individual or too consequential to be answered by an automated tool. For the matters listed in section 5.2, the HR assistant must not give a decision, an assessment of the individual case, or advice on what the outcome should be. It may do only the following:

- Explain in general terms what the relevant policy says and cite it.
- Explain the channels available and the response times.
- Offer to prepare a draft ticket in the correct category for the employee to review and confirm.
- In an emergency, tell the employee to contact the emergency services first.

### 5.2 The list

The following must always be escalated to People Operations staff:

1. Harassment, sexual harassment, discrimination, bullying or retaliation (POL-012).
2. Threats, violence or any safety concern.
3. Medical conditions, medical leave and disability, including requests for accommodation.
4. Mental health crises. The HR assistant should also give the Employee Assistance Program details (POL-009 section 8.1).
5. Pay disputes, suspected payroll errors and changes to bank details.
6. Disciplinary matters, performance improvement plans and the end of employment.
7. Legal claims, legal threats and requests from lawyers or authorities.
8. Immigration status and work authorization.
9. Any request for information about another employee that the requester is not entitled to see (section 7).
10. Any question where the published policies do not contain the answer and the outcome would affect the employee's pay, benefits or employment.

### 5.3 Why

A wrong or careless automated answer on these matters could cause real harm to a person or create legal risk for Fernhill. The HR assistant is designed to recognize these matters and hand them to a person, not to try to resolve them.

## 6. Escalation Path

### 6.1 Within People Operations

1. **People Operations generalist** or the specialist named in section 3 handles the ticket first.
2. **People Operations manager** is brought in if the ticket is not resolved by the target date, or if the employee asks for a review.
3. **Chief People Officer** decides exceptions to policy and any case involving a director or above.

### 6.2 Asking for a review

If you disagree with a decision, reply on the ticket within 10 business days and ask for a review. A People Operations manager who was not involved in the original decision reviews it and replies within 5 business days. The review decision is final, except for Workplace Concern cases, which have their own appeal process in the case outcome letter.

### 6.3 Exceptions to policy

An exception to a policy can be approved only by the person named in that policy, or otherwise by the Chief People Officer. Managers cannot approve exceptions that a policy reserves for People Operations or a director. The HR assistant cannot approve any exception. It can explain whether an exception route exists and help prepare the request.

## 7. Privacy and Access to Employee Information

### 7.1 Your own record

You may see your own employee profile, time off balances, benefits elections and tickets.

### 7.2 Managers

A manager may see the following for their direct reports only: job title, department, employment classification, work arrangement, assigned office, start date, time off balances and time off requests. A manager may not see a direct report's benefits elections, medical information, bank details or Workplace Concern tickets.

### 7.3 Everyone else

No employee may see another employee's record unless they are that employee's manager or a member of People Operations acting in their role. Requests for information about a colleague are declined. This includes requests that seem harmless, such as asking how much PTO a teammate has left.

### 7.4 The HR assistant

The HR assistant applies these access rules automatically, based on who is signed in. It will not look up another person's record at the request of someone who is not entitled to see it, whatever reason is given.

## 8. Tickets Prepared by the HR Assistant

### 8.1 Drafts and confirmation

The HR assistant can prepare a draft ticket or a draft message to a manager. It shows you the full draft, including the category, the summary and any details from your record. Nothing is created or sent until you confirm. You may edit or cancel the draft.

### 8.2 What the HR assistant cannot do

The HR assistant cannot approve a request, change a record, cancel a ticket that has been submitted, or contact anyone on your behalf without your confirmation. Approval always comes from the person named in the relevant policy.

### 8.3 Record of actions

Every ticket created through the HR assistant records that it was created with the assistant, and the time at which the employee confirmed it.

## 9. Closing a Ticket

A ticket is closed when the request has been answered or decided and the employee has been told. You may reopen a ticket within 10 business days of closure by replying to it. Tickets and their history are kept for 7 years, or longer where the law requires.

## 10. Feedback

After a ticket is closed, you are invited to rate the response. People Operations reviews ratings each month and publishes its average response times each quarter.

## 11. Questions

Questions about this procedure may be raised as a ticket under "Policy Question" or by email to people@fernhill.example.
