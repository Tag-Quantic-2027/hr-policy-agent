# Performance Management Policy

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy describes how goals are set, how feedback is given, how performance is reviewed and rated, how promotions are decided, and what happens when performance falls below what the role requires. It applies to full-time and part-time employees who have completed the probationary period. Performance during the probationary period is managed through the 30, 60 and 90 day check-ins in the Onboarding and Probation Procedure (POL-011) and not through this policy. Interns receive feedback through the internship program. Contractors are managed under their contract.

The HR assistant can explain this policy. It does not discuss any individual's rating, review or performance improvement plan, and it must hand those matters to People Operations (POL-013 section 5.2).

## 2. The Performance Year

The performance year is the calendar year. The main dates are:

| Step | When | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Goals agreed and recorded | By January 31 | Employee and manager |
| Mid-year check-in | July | Employee and manager |
| Self-assessment | First two weeks of December | Employee |
| Manager assessment | By January 15 | Manager |
| Calibration | Second half of January | Directors and People Operations |
| Ratings confirmed and shared | By February 15 | Manager |
| Salary review | March, effective April 1 | See POL-014 section 5 |

An employee whose start date is after October 1 does not receive a rating for that year. Their first rated year is the following year.

## 3. Goals and Regular Feedback

### 3.1 Goals

Each employee has between 3 and 5 goals for the year. A goal states the outcome, how it will be measured and by when. Goals are recorded in the HR portal and may be changed during the year with the manager's agreement when priorities change.

### 3.2 One-to-one meetings

Managers hold a one-to-one meeting with each direct report at least once every two weeks. If your manager regularly cancels one-to-one meetings, you may raise it with your manager's manager or with People Operations.

### 3.3 Feedback

Feedback should be given close to the event, should be specific and should be given in private when it is critical. A concern about performance should never be raised for the first time in the annual review.

## 4. Ratings

### 4.1 The four ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exceeds expectations | Consistently delivered well beyond the goals and the expectations of the level |
| Meets expectations | Delivered the goals and met the expectations of the level |
| Partially meets expectations | Delivered some goals, with clear gaps against the expectations of the level |
| Does not meet expectations | Did not deliver the goals or meet the expectations of the level |

### 4.2 Calibration

Proposed ratings are reviewed in a calibration meeting for each department so that ratings mean the same thing across teams. There is no fixed quota for any rating.

### 4.3 Where ratings are used

Other policies refer to the most recent rating:

- Eligibility to become fully remote needs "Meets expectations" or higher, where a review exists (POL-004 section 4.1).
- Internal transfers need "Meets expectations" or higher (POL-021 section 6).
- International transfers need "Meets expectations" or higher (POL-022 section 5).
- The salary review and the annual bonus take the rating into account (POL-014 sections 5 and 6).

### 4.4 Who can see a rating

A rating can be seen by the employee, their manager, the managers above them and People Operations. Ratings are Confidential data (POL-006 section 3.1). The HR assistant cannot see ratings.

### 4.5 If you disagree with your rating

Add a written comment to the review in the HR portal within 10 business days of the rating being shared. Your department director reviews the rating with People Operations and replies within 10 business days. The director's decision is final.

## 5. Leave and Part-Year Service

An employee who was on leave for part of the year is assessed on the time worked. An employee who worked fewer than 3 months of the performance year because of leave is not rated for that year and is treated as "Meets expectations" for the purposes of section 4.3. Taking parental leave, medical leave or any other protected leave must never reduce a rating.

## 6. When Performance Falls Below Expectations

### 6.1 Early conversation

When a manager sees a sustained gap, the manager raises it in a one-to-one meeting, gives examples, agrees what needs to change and by when, and records a short note in the HR portal. Most gaps are resolved at this stage.

### 6.2 Performance improvement plan

If the gap continues, the manager may start a performance improvement plan with the agreement of People Operations. A manager cannot start a plan alone. A plan must:

- State the specific gaps, with examples.
- State what success looks like in measurable terms.
- State the support the employee will receive, such as training, coaching or clearer priorities.
- Run for 60 calendar days.
- Include a review meeting at least every 2 weeks.

A plan may be extended once, by up to 30 calendar days, with the agreement of People Operations.

### 6.3 What changes while a plan is active

While you are on a performance improvement plan:

- You are not eligible for the Work From Anywhere program (POL-005 section 3). Approved requests that have not yet started are paused and are reviewed again when the plan ends.
- You may not apply for an internal transfer (POL-021 section 6) or an international transfer (POL-022 section 5).
- Your salary review is deferred (POL-014 section 5.4).
- You may not start a compressed work week (POL-017 section 5).

A plan does not affect paid time off, sick leave, benefits, the learning budget or your work arrangement. You may take approved PTO during a plan. The plan is paused for any absence of more than 5 consecutive working days and continues when you return.

### 6.4 Outcomes

At the end of the plan there are three possible outcomes:

1. The plan is completed successfully and closed. The restrictions in section 6.3 end on the same day.
2. The plan is extended once, as described in section 6.2.
3. The plan is not completed successfully. People Operations reviews the case before any decision about the employee's role or employment is made or communicated.

### 6.5 Confidentiality

A performance improvement plan is known only to the employee, the manager, the managers above them and People Operations. It is recorded in the employee profile so that the eligibility rules in section 6.3 can be applied. The HR assistant can apply those eligibility rules for the employee who is signed in. It does not discuss the plan, its content or its likely outcome, and it never reveals to anyone else that a plan exists.

### 6.6 Misconduct is different

This section is about performance, meaning the quality and quantity of work. Misconduct, such as dishonesty or harassment, is handled under the Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy (POL-012) and does not require a performance improvement plan.

## 7. Promotions

### 7.1 Promotion dates

Promotions take effect twice a year, on April 1 and October 1.

### 7.2 Criteria

A promotion to the next job level is considered when all of the following are true:

- The employee has been performing consistently at the next level for at least 6 months.
- The employee has spent at least 12 months at the current level.
- The most recent rating is "Meets expectations" or higher.
- The employee is not on a performance improvement plan.
- For levels L6 and above, there is a business need for a role at that level.

### 7.3 Process

The manager writes a promotion case and the department director decides, after review by a promotion panel for the department. Employees may ask their manager at any time what they would need to demonstrate to be promoted. Managers must answer that question in writing within 10 business days.

### 7.4 Promotion and pay

Pay on promotion is covered by POL-014 section 5.5.

## 8. Managers' Responsibilities

Managers must:

- Agree goals with each direct report by January 31.
- Hold one-to-one meetings at least every two weeks.
- Complete the manager assessment by January 15 and share the confirmed rating by February 15.
- Involve People Operations before starting a performance improvement plan.
- Keep performance information confidential.

A manager who has not completed performance reviews for their team by the deadline is reminded by People Operations, and the department director is told after 5 business days.

## 9. Record Keeping

Goals, reviews and ratings are kept in the HR portal for the period set out in the Employee Data Privacy Policy (POL-020) section 6.

## 10. Questions

Questions about how the process works may be raised as a ticket under "Policy Question". Questions about your own rating or plan should go to your manager first, and then to People Operations directly at people@fernhill.example.
