# Compensation and Payroll Policy

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy explains how pay is set, when and how employees are paid, how pay is reviewed, and what to do when something about pay is wrong. It applies to full-time employees, part-time employees and interns. Independent contractors are paid under their contract and submit invoices to Finance. Nothing in this policy applies to contractors.

Individual pay is never discussed by the HR assistant. The HR assistant can explain the rules in this policy. It cannot see anyone's salary, bank details or pay statements, and it must hand pay disputes and suspected pay errors to a person (POL-013 section 5.2).

## 2. How Pay Is Set

### 2.1 Job levels

Every role has a job level from L1 to L8. The level describes the scope of the role, not the person. The level of your role is shown in your offer letter and in the HR portal.

### 2.2 Pay bands

Each job level has a pay band with a minimum, a midpoint and a maximum. Pay bands are set separately for each country of employment and are reviewed every January against market data. A person new to a level is normally paid between the minimum and the midpoint of the band.

### 2.3 Pay zones

In the United States and Canada, pay bands also depend on a pay zone, which is based on where you live:

| Country | Zone A | Zone B |
|---|---|---|
| United States | New York, Washington state and Illinois | All other registered states |
| Canada | Ontario and British Columbia | Alberta |

Portugal has a single pay zone. If you move home to a location in a different pay zone, your pay band changes from the date of the move, and your pay may be adjusted (POL-004 section 6.3). People Operations tells you the effect before you commit to the move.

### 2.4 Part-time employees and interns

Part-time employees are paid the full-time rate for their level, prorated by scheduled hours per week. Interns are paid an hourly rate set for the internship program and are not placed in a pay band.

## 3. When and How You Are Paid

### 3.1 Pay dates

| Country of employment | Frequency | Pay dates |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Twice a month | The 15th and the last business day of the month |
| Canada | Twice a month | The 15th and the last business day of the month |
| Portugal | Monthly | The 25th of the month |

If a pay date falls on a weekend or a company holiday, pay is issued on the business day before. Employees in Portugal also receive the holiday and Christmas subsidies required by Portuguese law. Paid time off accrues as described in POL-002 section 2.2 for every country of employment.

### 3.2 Method of payment

Pay is sent by bank transfer to the account recorded in the HR portal. Fernhill does not pay by check or in cash.

### 3.3 Pay statements

A pay statement is published in the HR portal on each pay date. It shows gross pay, each deduction and net pay. Pay statements are Restricted data (POL-006 section 3.1) and can be seen only by you and by the Payroll team.

### 3.4 Changing your bank details

You change your bank details yourself, in the HR portal, after confirming your identity with multi-factor authentication. A change must be saved at least 5 business days before a pay date to apply to that pay date.

Bank details are never changed by email, by chat, by telephone or through the HR assistant. If you receive a message asking you to confirm or change your bank details, treat it as a security incident and report it (POL-006 section 8).

## 4. Deductions

Fernhill deducts the taxes and social contributions required by law in your country of employment, your contributions to the retirement plan, your share of health plan costs (POL-009 section 3.3), and any other deduction you have agreed to in writing. Fernhill does not make any other deduction from pay without your written agreement, except where this policy or another policy states that an amount may be recovered and local law allows it.

## 5. Annual Salary Review

### 5.1 Timing

Salaries are reviewed once a year. Managers propose changes in March, and approved changes take effect on April 1.

### 5.2 Who is included

You are included in the annual salary review if your start date is on or before October 1 of the previous year. Employees who started after that date are first reviewed in the following year. Interns are not included.

### 5.3 What is considered

A salary increase is not automatic. The review considers your performance rating for the previous year (POL-015 section 4), your position in the pay band, and the budget approved for the year. An employee whose pay is already at the maximum of the band does not receive an increase to base pay unless the band moves.

### 5.4 Employees on a performance improvement plan

If you are on a performance improvement plan on April 1, your salary review is deferred. It is completed within 30 calendar days of the plan ending successfully, and any increase takes effect from the first day of the following month. It is not backdated.

### 5.5 Promotions

A promotion to a higher job level takes effect on April 1 or October 1 (POL-015 section 7). Pay on promotion is at least the minimum of the new band.

## 6. Annual Bonus

### 6.1 Eligibility

Full-time and part-time employees at levels L3 and above take part in the annual bonus plan. Employees in the Sales department take part in the sales commission plan instead and receive a separate plan document. Interns and contractors are not eligible for any bonus.

### 6.2 Target and payment

The bonus target is a percentage of base pay set for each job level. The amount paid depends on company results and on your performance rating. The bonus for a year is paid with the last pay date in March of the following year.

### 6.3 Conditions

- You must be employed by Fernhill on the payment date. A bonus is not paid to someone who has left or who has given notice of resignation before the payment date, unless local law requires it.
- If you joined during the year, the bonus is prorated by the number of full months worked. Employees who join on or after October 1 are not eligible for that year.
- Part-time employees receive a bonus based on their prorated base pay.
- A rating of "Does not meet expectations" means no bonus is paid for that year.

## 7. Overtime and Additional Pay

Overtime rules are set out in the Working Hours, Overtime and On-Call Policy (POL-017). Overtime must be approved in advance. Approved overtime and on-call allowances are paid with the pay date that follows the end of the month in which they were worked.

## 8. Pay Errors

### 8.1 Reporting

If you think your pay is wrong, raise a ticket in the HR portal under the category "Payroll Question" as soon as you notice. State the pay date and what you believe is wrong. Do not include your bank account number in the ticket. A suspected error that affects the current pay period is priority P1 and receives a first response within 4 business hours (POL-013 section 4.1).

### 8.2 Underpayments

If Fernhill has underpaid you by USD 100 or more, or the equivalent in local currency, the correction is paid within 3 business days of the error being confirmed. A smaller underpayment is corrected in the next regular pay.

### 8.3 Overpayments

If Fernhill has overpaid you, you must tell Payroll. Payroll agrees a repayment schedule with you in writing before any amount is recovered. Recovery is normally spread over the same number of pay periods as the overpayment covered.

### 8.4 What the HR assistant does

The HR assistant does not investigate pay errors and does not estimate what you should have been paid. It can explain this section and prepare a draft ticket under "Payroll Question" for you to confirm.

## 9. Pay During Absences

- Paid time off, company holidays and floating holidays are paid at your normal rate (POL-002 and POL-003).
- Sick leave, parental leave, bereavement leave and jury duty are paid as described in POL-010.
- Unpaid personal leave is not paid (POL-010 section 7.3).
- Working from another location under POL-005 does not change your pay. You continue to be paid by your country of employment.

## 10. Salary Advances and Loans

Fernhill does not offer salary advances or loans. In a serious personal emergency, an employee who has completed the probationary period may ask for a hardship advance of up to half of one month's net pay. A hardship advance needs approval from the Chief People Officer and is repaid from pay over no more than 6 months. Requests are made confidentially to People Operations and not through the HR assistant.

## 11. Pay Confidentiality

Fernhill treats individual pay as Restricted data. Managers see the pay of their direct reports only during the salary review and only in the review tool. You are free to discuss your own pay with anyone you choose. No one may be penalized for doing so. You may not share another person's pay information that you learned through your role.

## 12. Tax Forms and Proof of Income

Year-end tax forms are published in the HR portal by the date required by law in your country of employment. If you need proof of employment or income for a lender or landlord, see the Employee Data Privacy Policy (POL-020) section 7.

## 13. Final Pay

Final pay when employment ends is described in the Offboarding and Separation Policy (POL-018) section 6.

## 14. Questions

General questions about how this policy works may be raised under "Policy Question". Anything about your own pay goes under "Payroll Question" and is answered by a payroll specialist. See POL-013 for response times.
