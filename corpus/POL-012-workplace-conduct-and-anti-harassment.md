# Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Fernhill Technologies is committed to a workplace where everyone is treated with respect. This policy sets out the standards of conduct we expect, defines harassment and discrimination, and explains how to raise a concern and what happens when you do.

This policy applies to full-time employees, part-time employees, interns and independent contractors. It applies in Fernhill offices, when working remotely, in company chat and email, at work events, during business travel and on social media where the conduct affects colleagues.

## 2. Standards of Conduct

Everyone at Fernhill is expected to:

- Treat colleagues, customers and suppliers with courtesy and respect.
- Act honestly, including in expense claims, time records and reports.
- Protect company and customer information (POL-006).
- Avoid conflicts of interest and declare any that arise (section 6).
- Follow lawful and reasonable instructions from their manager.
- Speak up when they see conduct that breaches this policy.

## 3. Harassment, Discrimination and Bullying

### 3.1 Discrimination

Discrimination is treating someone less favorably because of a protected characteristic. Protected characteristics include age, disability, gender identity, marital or family status, national origin, pregnancy, race, religion or belief, sex and sexual orientation, and any other characteristic protected by local law. Fernhill does not tolerate discrimination in hiring, pay, promotion, work assignment or any other decision.

### 3.2 Harassment

Harassment is unwelcome conduct that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading or offensive environment. It can be a single serious incident or a pattern of behavior. Examples include offensive jokes or comments, unwelcome remarks about appearance, displaying offensive material, mocking an accent, and repeatedly excluding someone.

### 3.3 Sexual harassment

Sexual harassment is unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature. Examples include unwelcome touching, sexual comments or jokes, requests for sexual favors, sending sexual images or messages, and repeated invitations after a person has said no. Sexual harassment is a serious breach of this policy whoever commits it and wherever it happens.

### 3.4 Bullying

Bullying is repeated behavior that is intended to intimidate, humiliate or undermine a person. Reasonable management, such as setting goals, giving honest feedback or managing poor performance fairly, is not bullying.

### 3.5 Intent

Conduct can be harassment even if the person did not intend it. What matters is the effect on the person and whether it was reasonable for the conduct to have that effect.

## 4. Raising a Concern

### 4.1 You have several channels

You may raise a concern through any of the following. You choose the channel. You do not have to raise it with your manager first, and you do not need anyone's permission.

1. **Your manager,** or any manager you trust.
2. **People Operations,** by email at people@fernhill.example or through a ticket in the HR portal under the category "Workplace Concern". Workplace Concern tickets are visible only to the People Operations case team, never to your manager.
3. **The Ethics Line,** an independent service available 24 hours a day by phone and web form. You may report anonymously. The details are in the HR portal and on posters in every office.

### 4.2 What to include

It helps to say what happened, when and where, who was involved and who may have seen it. You do not need proof to raise a concern, and you do not need to be sure that the conduct breaches this policy.

### 4.3 If you are in immediate danger

If you or anyone else is in immediate danger, contact the local emergency services first. Then tell People Operations.

### 4.4 The role of the HR assistant

The HR assistant can explain this policy and the reporting channels. It does not investigate, judge whether conduct was harassment, or advise on what the outcome should be. If you describe a concern to the HR assistant, it will explain your options and, if you want, prepare a draft Workplace Concern ticket for you to review and confirm. Nothing is sent to People Operations unless you confirm it. See POL-013 section 5.

## 5. What Happens After You Raise a Concern

### 5.1 Acknowledgment

People Operations acknowledges every concern within 2 business days and tells you who your case contact is.

### 5.2 Informal resolution

Where you want it and where it is appropriate, People Operations can help resolve a concern informally, for example through a facilitated conversation. Informal resolution is never required and is not used for sexual harassment or other serious allegations.

### 5.3 Investigation

Serious or unresolved concerns are investigated by a People Operations investigator who has no connection to the people involved. Fernhill aims to complete an investigation within 30 calendar days of the concern being raised. You are told if it will take longer. Both the person raising the concern and the person it is about may be accompanied to meetings by a colleague.

### 5.4 Outcome

You are told when the investigation is complete and, as far as confidentiality allows, what action has been taken. Outcomes range from no action, through training and written warnings, to the end of employment or contract.

### 5.5 Confidentiality

Concerns are shared only with the people who need to know in order to investigate and respond. Fernhill cannot promise complete confidentiality, because the person a concern is about usually needs to be told enough to respond. Records are stored as Restricted data.

### 5.6 No retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, or who takes part in an investigation, is prohibited and is itself a serious breach of this policy. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, exclusion, a poor review given as punishment, and threats. Report suspected retaliation through any channel in section 4.1.

## 6. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest exists when your personal interests could affect, or appear to affect, your judgment at work. Examples are a close relationship with a supplier, a second job with a competitor, and managing a family member or partner.

Declare any conflict to your manager and People Operations as soon as you become aware of it. Most conflicts can be managed once they are declared. Outside work for another organization needs your manager's written approval and must not use Fernhill time, equipment or information. A romantic relationship between a manager and anyone in their reporting line must be declared to People Operations so that the reporting line can be changed.

## 7. Gifts and Hospitality

You may accept gifts and hospitality from customers and suppliers only if they are modest and occasional. The limits are:

- A gift worth up to USD 100 from any one source in a calendar year may be accepted. Tell your manager.
- A gift worth more than USD 100 must be declined or handed to People Operations.
- Cash and gift cards may never be accepted, whatever the amount.
- Hospitality, such as a meal or an event ticket, worth more than USD 200 per person needs approval from your department director in advance.

Gifts given to customers follow the limits in POL-007 section 5.1. Never give or accept anything intended to influence a business decision improperly.

## 8. Alcohol and Drugs

Do not work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs. Alcohol may be served at company events in moderation. You are responsible for your conduct at events and this policy applies in full. If you are taking prescribed medication that could affect your ability to work safely, speak to People Operations in confidence.

## 9. Health and Safety

Report hazards, accidents and near misses in an office to the office manager on the same day. Violence and threats of violence are never tolerated. Weapons are not permitted in any Fernhill office or at any company event.

## 10. Breaches of This Policy

A breach of this policy may lead to disciplinary action, up to and including the end of employment or contract. Managers who know about harassment or discrimination and do nothing are also in breach of this policy. A manager who receives a concern must pass it to People Operations within 1 business day, even if the employee asks them not to act.

## 11. Questions

General questions about this policy may be raised as a ticket under "Policy Question". To raise a concern, use any of the channels in section 4.1.
