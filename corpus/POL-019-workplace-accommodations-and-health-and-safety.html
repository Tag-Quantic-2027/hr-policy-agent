Workplace Accommodations and Health and Safety Policy

Fernhill Technologies is a fictional company created for a course project. Every person, office and figure in this document is invented.

1. Purpose and Scope

This policy has two parts. Sections 2 to 6 explain how to ask for an accommodation, which is an adjustment to your work or workplace because of a disability, a medical condition, pregnancy or a religious practice. Sections 7 to 11 set out the health and safety rules for Fernhill offices and for working at home.

This policy applies to full-time employees, part-time employees and interns. Job applicants may ask for an accommodation during the hiring process by contacting People Operations. Independent contractors should speak to their Fernhill sponsor.

Where local law gives an employee a greater entitlement than this policy, local law applies.

2. Accommodations: The Principles

Fernhill provides reasonable accommodations so that every employee can do their job and take part in working life. An accommodation is reasonable if it removes or reduces a barrier without causing serious difficulty or cost to the business.

You do not need to use any special words to ask for an accommodation. You do not need to tell your manager about a medical condition. You may go directly to People Operations.

The HR assistant does not assess accommodation requests, does not ask about medical conditions and does not say whether a request is likely to be approved. It may explain this policy and prepare a draft ticket in the category "Accommodation Request" for you to review and confirm (POL-013 section 5.2).

3. How to Request an Accommodation

3.1 Raise a ticket

Raise a ticket in the HR portal under the category "Accommodation Request". Describe the barrier you are facing and, if you know it, the adjustment that would help. Do not attach medical documents to the ticket and do not describe your diagnosis in the ticket. A case manager will tell you how to send any documents securely.

3.2 Response

Accommodation Request tickets are at least priority P2. A People Operations case manager replies within 1 business day (POL-013 section 4).

3.3 The conversation

The case manager speaks with you to understand what you need. If the need is not obvious, the case manager may ask for a note from a doctor or other professional. The note needs to state the limitation and the adjustment recommended. It does not need to state a diagnosis.

3.4 Decision

People Operations aims to give a decision within 10 business days of receiving the information it needs. If the adjustment you asked for is not possible, the case manager must propose an alternative and explain the reason in writing.

3.5 Interim measures

If you cannot work safely or effectively while a request is being considered, tell the case manager. Simple interim measures, such as working from home or a change of desk, can be put in place within 2 business days.

4. Examples of Accommodations

  • Ergonomic equipment, such as a specialist chair, a sit-stand desk or an adapted keyboard. Fernhill pays for equipment provided as an accommodation. It is not charged to the home office stipend (POL-007 section 6.4).
  • Assistive software, such as screen readers or dictation tools, installed by IT on your company laptop.
  • A change to working hours or break times, including different core hours.
  • A change to anchor days or to the work arrangement for a hybrid employee.
  • A quiet workspace or permission to use noise-cancelling headphones.
  • Time and a private space for prayer, and schedule changes for religious observance.
  • A private room for expressing milk, available in every Fernhill office.
  • A service animal in the office.

An accommodation does not change the rules on where Restricted data may be accessed (POL-006 section 3.4) or the limits on working from another country (POL-005). A request to work from another country for medical or family reasons is handled as an exception request under POL-005 section 5.3.

5. Confidentiality of Accommodation Requests

Medical information is Restricted data. It is seen only by the People Operations case team. Your manager is told what adjustment has been agreed and anything they need to do to make it work. Your manager is not told the medical reason. Colleagues are told nothing unless you choose to tell them.

A manager who learns about an employee's medical condition must not record it, must not share it and should encourage the employee to contact People Operations.

6. Review, Temporary Accommodations and Appeals

6.1 Temporary accommodations

An accommodation for a temporary condition, such as recovery from an injury, is agreed for a stated period of up to 12 weeks and may be renewed.

6.2 Review

Long-term accommodations are reviewed once a year, or sooner if your role, your location or your needs change. A review is a check that the accommodation still works. It is not a new application.

6.3 If you disagree

If you disagree with a decision, ask for a review as described in POL-013 section 6.2. A People Operations manager who was not involved in the original decision reviews it and replies within 5 business days.

6.4 No retaliation

No one may be treated less favorably because they asked for an accommodation. Retaliation is a breach of the Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy (POL-012).

7. Health and Safety: Responsibilities

Fernhill is responsible for providing a safe place to work. Each office has an office manager who is the health and safety lead for that office. Every employee is responsible for working safely, following instructions from fire wardens and first aiders, and reporting hazards.

Each office has at least 2 trained first aiders and 2 fire wardens for every floor. Their names are displayed at reception and in the company chat tool. Volunteers receive training during working time.

8. Reporting Hazards, Accidents and Injuries

8.1 In an emergency

Call the local emergency number first. Then tell the office manager or, outside office hours, the IT Security hotline, which is staffed 24 hours a day and can reach the on-call facilities contact.

8.2 Hazards and near misses

Report a hazard, an accident or a near miss in an office to the office manager on the same day (POL-012 section 9).

8.3 Work-related injury or illness

Report any injury or illness caused by work to People Operations within 24 hours, even if it seems minor and even if it happened while working at home or traveling for business. People Operations explains how workers' compensation or the local equivalent applies. Late reporting can affect a claim.

8.4 Violence and threats

Violence and threats of violence are never tolerated. If you feel unsafe, leave the area and contact the emergency services. Threats are always handled by a person and never by the HR assistant (POL-013 section 5.2).

9. Offices

  • Wear your building pass. Do not let anyone follow you through a secure door without a pass.
  • Sign visitors in at reception and stay with them.
  • Fire drills take place twice a year in each office. Taking part is required.
  • Keep walkways and exits clear. Do not bring personal electrical heaters or kettles.
  • If you have an infectious illness, do not come to the office. Use sick leave, or work from home if you feel well enough and your manager agrees (POL-010 section 2).

10. Working at Home

10.1 Your workspace

Employees who work at home must have a safe working environment and may be asked to complete a home workspace self-assessment once a year (POL-004 section 5). Complete the self-assessment within 30 calendar days of being asked.

10.2 Ergonomic assessment

Any employee may ask for a free ergonomic assessment of their workspace, at home or in the office, once a year. The assessment is carried out by video call. Request it through the IT portal. An ergonomic assessment is advice. It is not an accommodation request, and no medical information is needed.

10.3 What the assessment can lead to

Standard items recommended by an assessment, such as an external monitor or keyboard, are requested from IT under POL-008 section 3. Furniture is bought with the home office stipend (POL-008 section 3.4). If the assessment shows a need linked to a medical condition, the assessor will suggest that you raise an Accommodation Request.

10.4 Injuries at home

An injury that happens while you are working at home during working hours is reported in the same way as an injury in the office (section 8.3).

11. Wellbeing and Mental Health

The Employee Assistance Program offers confidential counseling 24 hours a day, with up to 8 sessions per issue per year, from your first day (POL-009 section 8.1). Fernhill is not told who uses it.

If you are struggling with workload or stress, speak to your manager or to People Operations. Each office has trained mental health first aiders, listed in the company chat tool. A mental health first aider listens and points you to support. They do not give treatment and do not report the conversation to your manager.

If you or a colleague are in crisis or at risk of harm, contact the emergency services first.

12. Returning to Work After Illness or Injury

After an absence of more than 10 consecutive working days for illness or injury, a People Operations case manager agrees a return to work plan with you (POL-010 section 3). The plan may include a phased return of up to 4 weeks on reduced hours at full pay. A longer phased return is treated as a temporary accommodation under section 6.1.

13. Questions

Raise a ticket under "Accommodation Request" for anything about an adjustment for you. Raise a ticket under "Workplace Concern" for a safety concern. General questions about this policy go under "Policy Question". See POL-013 for response times.
